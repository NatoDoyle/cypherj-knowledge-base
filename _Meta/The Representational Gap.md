# The Representational Gap

## Summary

The Representational Gap is Justin Scott's term for **the irreducible distance between actuality and any rendering of actuality** — the structural fact that the body encounters reality only through aperture-mediated representation, and that representation is never identical to what it renders. It is not a flaw in perception, a temporary absence of information, or a wound waiting to be healed. It is *architectural*: a rendering that perfectly captured actuality would not be a rendering but actuality itself, and there would be no encounter, only identity. The gap is what makes everything that matters possible — **genuine contact** (two terms held distinct rather than absorbed), **curiosity** (a field with a remainder still worth entering), **development** (more signal left to metabolize), **meaning** (a living process rather than completed information), and **relationship** (another person who stays genuinely other rather than collapsing into a model of them). The gap becomes briefly visible when prediction collapses — betrayal, grief, awe, falling in love — opening a **vacuum**, the moment of maximum encounter potential and also maximum temptation to close it fast. It gets closed prematurely by a **coherence object**: an explanation that forms before encounter has finished informing it, then expands linguistically, recruits identity, and is maintained socially until a person processes a representation of their experience instead of the experience. It gets denied permanently by [[The False Observer]], the settled orientation in which representation relates to actuality on the aperture's behalf and the aperture itself stops arriving. The correct relationship to the gap is neither premature closure nor collapse into formlessness but the **developed capacity to remain inside it without an answer** — the deepest available defense against Meaning Death, and the room in which love, curiosity, and meaning were always going to have to happen.
