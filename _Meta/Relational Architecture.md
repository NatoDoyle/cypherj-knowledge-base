# Relational Architecture

## Summary

The Relational Architecture is Justin Scott's **complete structural account of what relationships are, what love is, and what makes them survive** — not advice on communication or conflict, but a model of what is actually happening when two people attempt sustained contact. It is governed by three principles, each answering a different question. The **Reality Principle** (*is the system open?*): every relational system lives or dies on one variable — whether it privileges its own continuity over the truth of what's happening inside it. The moment survival outranks accuracy, the system closes and becomes a maintenance operation; *a relationship is alive to the degree that truth retains authority over the structure built to contain it*. The **Love Principle** (*was the recognition real?*): love is not a feeling but **the non-substitutable recognition of a specific person's reality as fully real, and the irreversible reorganization that follows** — a key cut to a specific lock, discovered rather than chosen, structurally distinct from attachment (which transfers), care (which can coexist with a closed system), and devotion (which can run inside a performance). Its evidence is transformation: *if contact leaves no mark, it did not occur*. The **Convergence Principle** (*does the field hold?*): even real love in an open system can fail if the **basin** — the field produced by six axes (openness, capacity, mutual legibility, transformation, reciprocity, continuity) operating above threshold *multiplicatively* — cannot metabolize what recognition produces under pressure. When it drops below threshold the relationship is captured by a neighboring attractor: the Maintenance Basin, the Volatility Basin, the Closed Loop, One-Sided Adaptation, or the Dissolution Field. What people call "something that works" is not a feeling but a viable convergence basin. The three together produce what everyone is actually looking for — not proximity, performance, or the feeling of connection, but **contact**: one interior finding another fully real, and both being irrevocably changed by having been found.
