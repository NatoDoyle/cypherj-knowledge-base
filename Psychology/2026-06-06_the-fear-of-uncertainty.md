## The fear of uncertainty is one of the most expensive fears a person can have.

Because the cost isn’t comfort.

The cost is your life.

A lot of people think uncertainty is what they’re afraid of.

It’s not.

They’re afraid of what uncertainty feels like.

That shakiness.

That instability.

That sensation that something could happen and you don’t know what.

I used to feel it all the time.

The problem is that when you live from that fear long enough, certainty starts looking like salvation.

You want certainty about the relationship.

Certainty about the career.

Certainty about the future.

Certainty about yourself.

And eventually you begin organizing your entire life around obtaining something life was never designed to provide.

Because life is fundamentally uncertain.

You were never supposed to be certain.

You were supposed to be clear.

You were never supposed to be omniscient.

You were supposed to be coherent.

There’s a difference.

A coherent person doesn’t know how everything turns out.

They know what matters enough to move anyway.

And uncertainty runs deeper than most people realize.

### It exists before almost every category we use to navigate reality.

Before good and evil.

Before right and wrong.

Before success and failure.

Before wisdom and foolishness.

Before reward and consequence.

Because before you can know what something is, you have to encounter it.

You have to step into it.

You have to participate in it.

Which means uncertainty comes first.

The relationship is uncertain before it becomes good or bad.

The decision is uncertain before it becomes wise or foolish.

The path is uncertain before it becomes success or failure.

The uncertainty isn’t a flaw in reality.

It’s the boundary where reality stops being theory and starts becoming experience.

It’s the cost of encounter.

The price of participation.

The place where life stops being an idea and becomes your life.

But when fear takes over, something strange happens.

You stop making decisions.

You start conducting investigations.

You research.

You analyze.

You replay conversations.

You seek reassurance.

You wait for the perfect answer.

The perfect feeling.

The perfect sign.

And meanwhile years pass.

Because certainty keeps promising that life can begin tomorrow.

After one more answer.

After one more realization.

After one more guarantee.

But tomorrow keeps moving.

And life keeps happening without your permission.

Then the fear gets even more dangerous.

Because it stops looking like fear.

It starts looking like wisdom.

“I’m just being careful.”

“I’m thinking things through.”

“I’m waiting until I know enough.”

But underneath all of it is the same question:

> “What if I’m wrong?”

And the answer is terrifying.

**You might be.**

You might choose wrong.

You might trust the wrong person.

You might fail.

You might lose.

You might get your heart broken.

You might grieve.

You might love something that doesn’t last.

But every meaningful thing in life requires exactly those risks.

- Love requires uncertainty.
- Art requires uncertainty.
- Friendship requires uncertainty.
- Faith requires uncertainty.
- Growth requires uncertainty.
- Participation requires uncertainty.

Which brings me to something I wish more people understood.

Good and evil can become certainty machines.

Success and failure can become certainty machines.

People take experiences and turn them into verdicts.

**“I failed”** becomes **“I am a failure.”**

**“I got hurt”** becomes **“I am broken.”**

**“I am struggling”** becomes **“I am doomed.”**

Because certainty feels safer than not knowing.

Even when the certainty is cruel.

It has always deceived you.

You are not certainly a failure.

You are not certainly doomed.

You are not certainly broken.

You are not certainly behind.

You are not certainly unlovable.

But you do clearly hurt.

You do clearly miss them.

You do clearly feel exhausted.

You do clearly know what it’s like to stare at the ceiling wondering how you’re supposed to keep going.

That part is real.

And maybe that’s where we start.

Not with certainty.

With clarity.

Not with a verdict.

With honesty.

Because the goal was never to know everything.

The goal was never to remove uncertainty from your life.

The goal was to become clear enough to move through uncertainty without abandoning yourself.

And maybe that shakiness we spend so much time running from isn’t proof that something is wrong.

Maybe it’s what it feels like to stand at the edge of a future that hasn’t happened yet.

Because most of the time, uncertainty isn’t a sign that you’re lost.

It’s a sign that you’re alive.
