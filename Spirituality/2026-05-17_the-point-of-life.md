I get asked this question all the time.

## “What’s the point of life?”

But that’s the thing right there.

There is no point *to* life.

You are a point *in* life.

We all are.

That’s the first thing they stole from you.

They convinced you life was somewhere else.

Somewhere beyond you.

Hidden behind something you had not earned yet.

So now you sit inside existence asking existence for permission to exist.

A wave asking the ocean why water exists.

A heartbeat demanding a thesis before beating.

A fire asking for the point of burning.

You are not outside life examining it.

You ARE one of the places it’s happening.

And the reason this generation feels spiritually exhausted is because people stopped participating in life and started spectating themselves instead.

You don’t live first anymore.

You monitor first.

- “What’s the point?”
- “What’s my purpose?”
- “What’s the meaning?”
- “What’s the reason?”
- “Why continue?”
- “How do I keep living?”

Look at the structure.

The mind has turned existence into a courtroom.

Life is standing trial every morning.

You wake up interrogating reality before touching it.

And then people wonder why they feel disconnected from living.

You keep trying to solve life before entering it.

But trees never formed philosophies about photosynthesis before growing.

Rivers never located their purpose before flowing.

Stars explode without understanding cosmology.

And somehow humans became the one creature arrogant enough to believe existence must become fully explainable before participation is allowed.

> “What is my purpose?”

Same problem.

**You** purpose.

Purpose is not hidden behind your life.

Purpose is what your life does while touching other lives.

But you were taught to think like machinery.

Like you were manufactured toward a final assignment.

So people spend twenty years waiting to become valid enough to begin.

Waiting for the right identity.

The right certainty.

The right self.

The right destiny.

And this is why so many people gave up their capacity to be dreamers on the way to their dreams.

Because eventually the dream stopped being alive.

It became permission.

Permission to rest.

Permission to matter.

Permission to finally feel complete.

That’s why some people get everything they thought they wanted and still feel empty.

Because they spent their entire life trying to arrive somewhere instead of remaining present enough to live while moving toward it.

> “I need to find myself.”

Then who is the one searching?

You talk about yourself like misplaced luggage.

Like somewhere out there is a completed version of you waiting to be discovered instead of a living system still being formed through grief, love, failure, embarrassment, responsibility, touch, loss, creation, and survival.

Human beings are not uncovered.

They are accumulated.

And then comes the anesthesia spirituality.

> “Everything happens for a reason.”

No.

Sometimes people fail you.

Sometimes systems fail you.

Sometimes damage is damage.

And the reason that sentence sounds comforting is because randomness terrifies the nervous system.

Because if everything happens for a reason…  
then nobody abandoned you.

Nobody betrayed you.

Nobody chose themselves over your pain.

The sentence becomes a sedative.

A beautiful way to avoid grief.

Not every scar is a secret lesson.

Some are evidence.

> And then they told you happiness was the goal.

So now everybody’s surveilling themselves to death.

“Am I happy?”

“Why don’t I feel fulfilled?”

“What’s wrong with me?”

The happiest moments of your life probably happened while you forgot to monitor yourself entirely.

Because happiness was never supposed to become government.

It’s weather.

A sunset stops being beautiful the second you demand it remain permanently in the sky.

Waiting for happenstance to become permanent, you watched joy die right in front of you.

Then comes the loneliness masquerading as wisdom.

> “You have to love yourself first.”

Humans learn language from other humans.

Why would love emerge differently?

Some people only discovered they were lovable because somebody finally treated them like they were.

But modern culture keeps trying to turn human beings into closed-loop systems.

- Self-contained.
- Self-healing.
- Self-loving.
- Self-regulating.
- Self-sustaining.

No wonder everybody’s exhausted.

Human beings are collapsing trying to perform the labor of entire villages alone, attempting to carry entire ecosystems of support inside one exhausted body.

> And then comes closure.

The fantasy that pain must completely resolve before movement is allowed.

No.

Some doors never close.

You just stop bleeding when you pass them.

Healing is not always an ending.

Sometimes healing is carrying ambiguity without collapsing beneath it.

But your mind in spectator mode cannot tolerate unresolved existence.

It wants certainty.

Finality.

Completion.

Permanent answers.

It wants life solved before life is lived.

And when it cannot solve life, it starts converting pain into philosophy.

- “It hurts too much.”
- “It’s too hard.”
- “I can’t do this anymore.”
- “How am I supposed to keep living?”
- “I just can’t move on.”

And you’re not always supposed to.

Not from love.

Not from grief.

Not from betrayal.

Not from becoming.

Some things are not meant to be erased.

They are meant to become part of your structure without remaining the center of your collapse.

Healing is not always forgetting.

Sometimes it is learning how to carry memory without letting it devour your entire future.

You think you’re failing because the wound still exists.

But healed skin still remembers the cut.

Pain is not revelation.

Pain is intensity exceeding current carrying capacity.

But the mind takes temporary overwhelm and translates it into eternal truth.

**The body says:**  
“I am overloaded.”

**The spectator says:**  
“Existence itself has become impossible.”

That’s the catastrophe.

You think you are discovering truth when you are actually exhausted beyond language.

A storm feels infinite from inside it.

That does not make it permanent.

And you don’t want to hear this, but some of what’s crushing you was never supposed to become part of you permanently.

Some of you have spent so long trying to survive everything that you no longer know who you are outside of carrying it.

You keep calling it your personality.

Your body calls it emergency response.

And eventually the organism revolts.

Not because you are weak.

Because no nervous system was designed to indefinitely carry: unprocessed grief, economic instability, social fragmentation, continuous stimulation, algorithmic comparison, identity performance, survival fear, and emotional isolation all at once forever.

> “It’s too hard.”

Some of it IS too hard.

Not life itself.

The arrangement.

That distinction matters.

You are not broken because impossible weight feels impossible to carry.

Mountains require legs.

That’s difficulty and difficulty does exist.

But if somebody chained concrete to your spine and called it maturity, your collapse would not be proof that walking is impossible.

And some of you have been dragging entire architectures through your nervous system for so long that exhaustion started sounding like philosophy.

> “I can’t do this anymore.”

Correct.

You cannot continue carrying it the way you’ve been carrying it.

That is not the same thing as life becoming impossible.

Burnout is often the body revolting against prolonged self-abandonment.

The nervous system collapses pathways that became unsustainable.

That breakdown before work.

That crying in the bathroom.

That numbness.

That exhaustion.

That sudden inability to keep pretending.

That was not always weakness.

Sometimes that was your body trying to free you from an arrangement your mind kept calling normal.

But spectator mode renames the collapse: failure. worthlessness. meaninglessness. hopelessness.

Because once you’ve been trained to survive through performance, your own humanity starts feeling like dysfunction.

And the worst part is that sometimes you ignore your body and go anyway.

You get dressed anyway.

You answer the email anyway.

You clock in anyway.

You smile anyway.

You perform anyway.

Even while something inside you is collapsing hard enough to bring your entire body to tears.

And we all know why you walked out that door.

Rent.  
Fear.  
Survival.  
Obligation.  
Health insurance.  
Children.  
Debt.  
Expectation.  
The terror of falling behind.  
The terror of becoming disposable.

But after enough years inside systems that force human beings to override themselves for survival, you stop calling it coercion.

You call it maturity.

So even your own pain starts sounding irresponsible to you.

You think you are failing life.

Your organism is rejecting conditions no living thing was meant to endure indefinitely.

There are things you are going to have to put down just to be alive honestly.

Versions of yourself.

Roles.

Performances.

Obligations.

Identities built entirely around surviving environments you no longer belong to.

Some identities are just scar tissue that learned how to speak.

And maybe that’s the part nobody tells you.

You were never supposed to permanently solve life.

**You were supposed to live it.**

Because if you ever truly arrived at a final answer to:

“What’s the point?”

“What’s the meaning?”

“Why continue?”

“How do I keep living?”

you wouldn’t still be here asking.

You would’ve taken yourself away from us.

The solution was never safety.

The solution was never certainty.

The solution was never escape from grief, or vulnerability, or change, or love, or loss, or becoming.

**The solution is the danger.**

Because living things survive through movement.

Terminal things arrive at conclusions.

And when you finally stop trying to permanently solve life…

something strange happens.

Life starts touching you again.

Food tastes different.

Music reaches further into you.

You laugh without monitoring the laugh.

You rest without earning it first.

You stop interrogating every emotion like it’s evidence in a trial.

You stop treating uncertainty like an emergency.

You stop demanding permanence from beautiful things.

And suddenly moments start arriving whole again.

Not because suffering disappeared.

Not because grief disappeared.

Not because the world became fair.

But because you finally stopped standing outside your own existence demanding proof before participation.

And maybe that’s what peace actually is.

Not certainty.

Not permanent happiness.

Not the elimination of pain.

Just enough presence to let life touch you while it’s happening.
