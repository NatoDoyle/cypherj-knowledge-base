Before we begin, I need one thing from you. This is a requirement to even continue reading this.

**If you spend this entire piece deciding whether it is defending God or attacking God, you will not be able to participate in the investigation.**

It is that serious.

You will hear every sentence as evidence for a conclusion you already brought with you.

And you will miss what is happening.

That is the requirement to reading this.

This is not an argument for God.

This is not an argument against God.

For the next few minutes,

I need you to look at what human beings do when they encounter reality.

The word “**tree**.” The cognitive word “**tree**,” is not a tree.

The word “**water**” has never made anyone wet.

The word “**love**” has never held anyone.

A word is not the thing.

It is a sign.

A reference.

Something the mind uses to point beyond itself.

The word “God” is not God.

It is a sign pointing toward whatever human beings have been trying to indicate with that word for thousands of years.

That distinction is simple.

But almost everything that follows depends on whether you can keep it open.

Because something happens to every sign.

Not just religious signs.

Every sign.

At first, it points.

Then it gets repeated.

Then it gets explained.

Then the explanation gets explained.

Then people begin protecting the explanation.

Eventually, nobody notices that they stopped looking where it was pointing.

You have lived this. I want you to know.

### **You have lived this.**

You meet someone.

At first, you are discovering them.

You are listening for what surprises you.

You are watching their face.

You are learning what hurts them.

You are letting them exceed what you expected.

Then something happens.

They disappoint you.

You explain why.

They do it again.

You add another explanation.

Then another.

Years later, they walk into the room, and before they speak, you already know who they are.

You are not meeting them anymore.

You are meeting the architecture you built from every previous meeting.

They are standing in front of you.

But you are talking to the tower.

You have lived this with your parents.

You have lived this with your children.

You have lived this with people you love.

You have lived this with yourself.

At first, you asked:

**Who am I?**

Then you found an answer.

Then you repeated the answer.

Then you defended the answer.

Eventually, you stopped discovering yourself.

You started protecting your biography.

You stopped introducing yourself.

You started defending yourself.

You have lived this in school.

Learning became grades. Right?

Grades became transcripts.

Transcripts became resumes.

Resumes became identity.

Somewhere underneath all of it, there was once a person trying to understand something.

You have lived this at work.

A meeting was created because people needed to communicate.

Then the meeting needed an agenda.

Then the agenda needed a format.

Then the format needed rules.

Then somebody became responsible for enforcing the rules.

Years later, everyone is protecting the meeting.

Nobody remembers the problem the meeting was built to solve.

This keeps happening because the human mind cannot carry reality in full.

You do not remember every conversation you have ever had.

**You remember what it meant.**

You do not remember every moment someone loved you.

You remember:

**They love me.**

You do not remember every detail of the fire.

You remember:

**“Hot.”**

The first time you touch fire, reality teaches you.

The second time, you do not need to touch it again.

You have the word.

“Hot.”

That word is a miracle.

It allows you to carry an encounter without repeating the injury.

That is what representation does.

That is what memory does.

That is what language does.

Compression is not corruption.

Compression is survival.

It is mercy.

Without it, every human being would have to begin from nothing.

Every child would have to rediscover fire.

Every generation would have to rediscover grief.

Every civilization would have to rediscover everything it already learned.

Language lets the dead teach the living.

Tradition lets one life carry another.

A sign is not the enemy.

But every mercy creates a danger.

Because once reality has been compressed, it becomes cheaper to return to the compression than to return to reality.

The first time, reality teaches you.

After that, memory can teach you.

Then language.

Then culture.

Then authority.

Then institution.

Each floor costs less than the one beneath it.

Reality is expensive.

Representations are cheap.

Love is expensive.

Talking about love is cheap.

Knowing another person is expensive.

Knowing your idea of another person is cheap.

Science is expensive.

Quoting science is cheap.

Prayer is expensive.

Talking about prayer is cheap.

Being present is expensive.

Performing presence is cheap.

Being corrected is expensive.

Repeating what you already know is almost free.

That is why towers form.

Not because human beings are stupid.

Not because institutions are evil.

Not because religion failed.

## Towers form because towers are efficient.

Every floor says:

You do not have to go all the way back down.

We carried something up for you.

We summarized it.

We named it.

We organized it.

We preserved it.

And at first, that is an act of mercy.

Every tower begins as an act of mercy.

The catastrophe comes later.

The catastrophe begins when the tower becomes so efficient that nobody notices they are no longer paying the cost of contact.

Every new floor checks the floor beneath it.

The floor beneath it checks the one beneath that.

The argument checks the doctrine.

The doctrine checks the story.

The story checks the image.

The image checks the word.

Almost nobody checks the ground.

And because each layer can be internally consistent with the layer beneath it, the entire tower can become logically perfect while resting on nothing.

It can be airtight.

It can be ancient.

It can be beautiful.

It can be defended by scholars.

It can be inherited by millions.

And nobody inside it may know whether it still reaches the thing it was built to reach.

That is where the horror is.

## Not in the existence of the tower.

## In the loss of the staircase.

Because once the staircase is gone, the tower becomes indistinguishable from the ground.

And the higher people climb, the more violently they may defend what is beneath their feet.

Not because they are closer to reality.

Because they are farther from it.

Arbitrary floors need louder defenses.

The ground does not need defending in the same way.

Nobody has ever needed to start a war over the statement:

Something exceeds my understanding.

Wars begin higher up.

Over the correct name.

The correct image.

The correct rule.

The correct fabric.

The correct interpretation of an interpretation of a sentence spoken by someone whose original encounter disappeared centuries ago.

The fights become more vicious as the reference becomes more remote.

And this is why I can’t stop thinking about the Tower of Babel.

Not only as a story about architecture.

As a story about cognition.

A tower built upward by human beings who believed height could produce contact.

Floor after floor.

Language on top of language.

Structure on top of structure.

Every new level farther from the ground while becoming more dependent on everything below it.

> There is a Tower of Babel in every human mind.

Not built from brick.

Built from representation.

Memory on top of encounter.

Explanation on top of memory.

Identity on top of explanation.

Defense on top of identity.

And every mind is trying to reach something through the tower.

Trying to reach another person.

**Trying to reach itself.**

**Trying to reach truth.**

**Trying to reach God.**

Without realizing that the only place it ever met any of them was the ground.

You did not first know life as an explanation.

You participated in it.

You touched fire before you understood heat.

You loved before you could define love.

You grieved before you had a philosophy of death.

You looked at the night sky before anyone told you what it meant.

Participation came first.

The tower came later.

And I need to tell you something before this piece goes any further.

This piece is becoming a tower.

Right now.

Every sentence I give you is another floor.

Every distinction is another floor.

Every example is another floor.

Every time you think, I understand, another floor has been built.

One day, someone may repeat this piece without returning to the thing that produced it.

Someone may quote it.

Someone may defend it.

Someone may argue about the correct definition of the tower.

Someone may use this language to stop listening to another human being.

Someone may turn this investigation into the very thing it is warning you about.

Maybe you will.

Maybe I will.

That is how natural this is.

This explanation is not outside the mechanism.

It is standing inside it.

So do not believe this piece simply because it sounds complete.

Use it.

And the moment it stops returning you to reality, leave it behind.

## Now we can return to the word God.

Imagine the beginning.

Someone encounters something too large for their model.

Death.

Birth.

Love.

The night sky.

The sheer fact that anything exists.

No doctrine yet.

No institution.

No theology.

Only encounter.

Then the nervous system needs a handle.

God.

That is the first floor.

Compression.

Not corruption.

Then the word receives a pronoun.

He.

Now whatever exceeded every available category has entered gender.

Then he needs a location.

Up.

Now the ground of existence has an address.

Then he needs a temperament.

Pleased.

Angry.

Jealous.

Merciful.

Now the condition of existence has moods roughly analogous to a person’s.

Moods require explanations.

So he gets preferences.

Certain foods.

Certain days.

Certain clothes.

Certain bodies.

Certain people.

Preferences require enforcement.

So interpreters appear.

Then ranks.

Then institutions.

Then doctrine.

Doctrine creates contradictions.

Contradictions require reconciliation.

Reconciliations create more contradictions.

Committees gather to reconcile the reconciliations.

People divide over which reconciliation correctly protects the original word.

Then identity enters.

Now this is not merely what you believe.

It is who you are.

And once the tower becomes identity, any question about the tower feels like an attack on the ground itself.

Eventually, people can spend their entire lives defending a platform so high above the original encounter that they would not recognize the ground if they reached it.

I have needed a word for that.

Godden.

Not God.

> **Godden.**

**Everything language, image, story, doctrine, institution, memory, commerce, fear, hope, and identity built around the word until defending what accumulated around God became indistinguishable from defending God.**

That is the illusion.

Not necessarily God.

The illusion is that the tower became the ground.

The illusion is that speaking fluently about the sign means you have remained in contact with what it points toward.

The illusion is that certainty measures proximity.

The illusion is that climbing higher brings you closer.

It does not.

It only places more material between you and the place where the encounter began.

This does not mean the tower is worthless.

You need language.

You need memory.

You need tradition.

You need the knowledge of people who lived before you.

The answer cannot be to destroy every tower.

You would lose too much.

The question is whether the tower still contains a staircase.

Can the word still return you to encounter?

Can the ritual still release your grip?

Can the doctrine still survive being corrected by reality?

Can you still meet a person beneath your explanation of them?

Can you still stand before existence without immediately replacing it with what you already believe?

Can you descend?

Because if God was only ever encountered at the ground, then no amount of climbing will bring you closer.

And if this piece becomes something you carry instead of somewhere you return through, put it down.

Do not carry the tower.

Carry the staircase.
