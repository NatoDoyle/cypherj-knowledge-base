I’m going to need you to lock in.

## **Missing someone is not evidence of love.**

It’s evidence that you may have mistaken a prosthetic for a soulmate.

And that sentence always bothers people, because some of you built your entire understanding of love around how much losing someone hurt.

You thought:

- “I can’t stop thinking about them.”
- “I still dream about them.”
- “I can’t eat.”
- “I can’t sleep.”
- “I compare everyone else to them.”

This must be real.

but no.

Pain is not proof.

Withdrawal is not proof.

Obsession is not proof.

Intensity is not proof.

Sometimes your strongest relationship was just the relationship your dysfunction relied on the most.

That’s what a prosthetic relationship is:

> It’s when someone becomes responsible for emotional functions your life was supposed to develop internally.

So that other person you end up dating, guess what they become.

They become:

your self-worth

your emotional regulation

your confidence

your social life

your future fantasy

your escape from loneliness

your distraction from trauma

your proof that you matter

your nervous system stabilizer

And now you’re not loving them, you’re using them to help you walk through a life you don’t know how to carry alone.

And people confuse that dependency with destiny every single day.

Because your nervous system can bond to:

- chaos
- inconsistency
- being abandoned and chosen again
- sexual volatility
- validation scarcity
- fixing broken people
- childhood dysfunction that feels familiar

And because it feels massive you call it soulmates.

No.

Sometimes your deepest love was just the relationship that activated your oldest wound most efficiently.

And when that relationship ends people think they’re grieving the person.

Sometimes you’re grieving the function.

### That’s why phantom pain matters.

Your body can feel pain from a limb that no longer exists.

Not because the limb is still there—but because your nervous system got used to organizing around it.

That’s what some of your exes are.

They’re just phantom pain.

You don’t miss them—

you miss:

- who you were around them
- what they distracted you from
- what they regulated for you
- what they helped you avoid building

And until you tell the truth about that you’ll romanticize dependency and keep calling it rare love.

And this is where people panic.

Because they think this means love isn’t real.

No.

**Real love is just rarer than your coping mechanisms.**

Real love does not make your life smaller.

Real love does not become your entire identity.

Real love does not collapse you to prove its depth.

Real love sounds like:

**“I can carry myself. And I deeply value what we uniquely create together.”**

That’s love.

Not two people using each other as emotional wheelchairs. Get past that.

And some of you are not trying to get your ex back—you’re trying to get your prosthetic back.

That’s what you really want.

And your healing doesn’t begin when you stop missing them.

It begins when you rebuild the parts of your life you outsourced.
