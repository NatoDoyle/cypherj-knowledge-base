## One of the fastest ways to make a relationship boring is to finally believe you understand your partner.

I know it sounds ridiculous.

**Because we’re told the goal of love is understanding.**

Understand their needs.

Understand their trauma.

Understand their attachment style.

Understand how they communicate.

Understand who they are.

And understanding matters.

But the moment you believe you’ve figured someone out, you stop encountering them.

You start interacting with your understanding of them.

Think about the beginning of a relationship.

Everything is discovery.

Every conversation contains uncertainty.

Every date contains uncertainty.

Every reaction contains uncertainty.

You don’t know what they’re going to say.

You don’t know how they’ll respond.

You don’t know who they are yet.

The person is larger than your understanding of them.

That’s part of what makes them feel alive.

### Then the relationship works.

You learn them.

You map them.

You predict them.

You understand them.

And slowly understanding starts replacing encounter.

**You stop asking because you already know.**

**You stop listening because, guess what, you already know.**

**You stop wondering because you already know.**

**You stop discovering because of the exact same thing.**

You start dating like a data analyst.

Collecting information.

Building models.

Tracking patterns.

Drawing conclusions.

The relationship becomes less about meeting a person and more about managing a prediction.

### This is why so many good relationships become boring.

Not even bad relationships we’re discussing right now.

Good ones.

The communication is fine.

The love is real.

The loyalty is real.

Nothing is necessarily wrong.

You’ve just become historians of each other.

Caretakers of archived versions.

Experts on who the other person used to be.

Meanwhile the actual person keeps changing.

Now your partner becomes trapped behind your representation of them.

If they’re upset:

> “You’re doing that thing again.”

If they change:

> “That’s not like you.”

If they grow:

> “You’ve become different.”

No matter what they do, they have to pass through yesterday before they can reach you.

And eventually the relationship fills with ghosts.

Not actual people.

Versions.

Archived copies.

Historical renderings.

The woman he’s arguing with isn’t the woman standing in front of him.

**It’s the version he’s already concluded exists.**

The man she’s disappointed in isn’t the man standing there.

**It’s the version she’s been carrying for years.**

Two people can spend years together while feeling increasingly unseen.

Not because nobody is looking.

Because everybody is looking through old lenses.

That’s what happens, and it happens nearly every single time.

This is why relationships need a lot more than communication.

**They need encounter.**

Encounter is core over communication.

New territory.

New conversations.

New experiences.

New realities neither person already possesses.

Not because novelty is the goal.

Because people are alive.

And living things cannot be fully reduced to a conclusion.

The goal of love is not even to completely understand another person.

The goal of love is to keep meeting them.

Because the moment a person becomes fully reduced to your understanding of them, the relationship starts living inside a story.

And eventually two people can spend years together without realizing they’re no longer relating to each other.

They’re relating to the stories they’ve built about each other.

So here’s the question:

Are you still encountering your partner?

Or are you mostly interacting with your understanding of them?
