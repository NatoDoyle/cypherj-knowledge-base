## Nobody talks about what it’s like to watch someone become certain about you.

They’ll finish your sentence.

They’ll tell you what you meant.

They’ll answer the version of you they already know.

The only new thing you came to say never had a chance.

They stopped listening before you said it.

So you try again.

Different words.

Different examples.

Different angles.

You soften it.

You shorten it.

You explain the explanation.

Until one day you realize you’re no longer just trying to be understood.

You’re in the moment trying to escape history.

**You’re trying to convince them to let you out of yesterday.**

Trying to convince them that the person they’re responding to...

isn’t the person standing in front of them.

And that’s one of the loneliest experiences a human being can ever have.

To stand in front of someone who loves you and realize that they only relate with a version of you that you **already buried.**

So you stop trying.

Not because you’re still that person.

But because you’re exhausted by someone who keeps shaking hands with their own memory.

Most people don’t remember the last time they genuinely tried to explain the truth.

They don’t even remember.

Not because it wasn’t important.

Because nobody knew they were standing inside the last honest conversation they’d ever have.

So the conversation ends.

Life goes on.

You still call.

You still go to work.

You still come home.

You still sit at the dinner table.

You still show up to the meeting.

But something doesn’t.

The sentence you almost said.

The question you swallowed.

The correction that stayed behind your teeth.

The story that never left the parking lot.

The hand that started to rise before quietly falling back into your lap.

Children who still call their parents every week who quietly decided years ago that some parts of themselves would never come through that front door again.

Employees who still do excellent work, **excellent work**, whose best ideas never leave the notebook.

Leaders who believe they have an honest team while every difficult truth dies in someone’s chest before the meeting even begins.

Marriages that didn’t lose affection.

They lost the expectation that saying,

> **“That’s not what I meant...”**

would change anything.

Friendships that never ended.

They simply stopped discovering each other.

Families where everyone still talks but everyone already knows which subjects don’t exist inside those four walls.

A Patient who says,

> **“I understand...”**

then spend the drive home hoping the prescription explains itself.

Years pass.

Until one person finally learns how to listen.

They finally ask the question they should have asked.

They finally become curious enough.

Patient enough.

Present enough.

But development has **a cruelty.**

Sometimes you become the person someone else needed after they’ve already learned not to bring that part of themselves to you anymore.

I’m trying to be serious with you.

Now two people are grieving the same conversation.

One grieves because they were never received.

The other grieves because they finally learned how right when there was no longer a journey left for the truth to make.

People ask,

> **“Why didn’t you tell me?”**

Sometimes the answer isn’t,

> **“I never tried.”**

Sometimes it’s,

> **“I did. Just not the day you finally knew how to hear me.”**

## Real understanding sounds different.

It slows down.

It asks, **“Is that what you mean?”**

It risks being wrong.

It waits.

It stays curious and it stays long enough for today to interrupt yesterday.

It doesn’t finish your sentence.

It waits for the part it’s never heard before.

That’s the whole point.

**If you’ve already decided who I am you can’t meet who I’m becoming.**

There is a cost to pretending to understand.

It isn’t just misunderstanding another person.

It’s slowly teaching them that the parts of themselves they most wanted you to know would survive better if they never had to reach you at all.
