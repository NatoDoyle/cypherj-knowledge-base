## So lot of people claim to be attractive

But how would you know?

Seriously.

> How would you know?

- You can know your height.
- Weight.
- Income.
- How many followers you have.
- How closely you match a beauty standard.

But attractive?

Attractive to who?

This part matters.

Attraction does not occur inside you.

It occurs inside the attracted.

Which means the entire conversation is already strange.

Because for years people have been saying:

“I’m attractive.”

“I’m unattractive.”

“I’m a high-value man.”

“I’m a high-value woman.”

Without noticing that they’re making claims about experiences happening inside other people.

### Imagine standing outside a restaurant and saying:

“The food tastes amazing.”

You haven’t tasted it.

You’ve seen the building.

You’ve seen the menu.

You’ve seen the line outside.

You’ve seen the reviews.

But taste occurs in the mouth of the person eating.

Attraction occurs in the experience of the attracted.

So what have we been measuring this whole time?

**Desirability**.

Not attraction.

And the difference matters more than people realize.

Because every time somebody says:

**“I’m unattractive.”**

The question should be:

What do you mean?

> Do you mean nobody has ever been attracted to you?

You don’t know that.

> Do you mean you don’t fit beauty standards?

That’s desirability.

> Do you mean you’ve been rejected?

That’s rejection.

> Do you mean you don’t feel wanted?

That’s loneliness.

> Do you mean the people around you don’t value your traits?

That’s a market.

Do you mean you can’t imagine somebody wanting you?

That’s hopelessness.

One sentence.

Six different realities.

Now let’s do the other side.

**“I’m attractive.”**

What do you mean?

> Do you mean people flirt with you?

That’s evidence.

> Do you mean you’re beautiful?

That’s desirability, again.

> Do you mean people desire you?

That’s demand.

> Do you mean attraction is possible for you?

That’s confidence.

> Do you mean you’ve received validation?

That’s validation.

> Do you mean people are attracted to you?

How would you know?

Again.

One sentence.

Six different realities.

The entire conversation starts falling apart the moment you ask people what they actually mean.

Because most people have never been talking about attraction.

They’ve been talking about beauty.

- Validation.
- Status.
- Loneliness.
- Rejection.
- Hope.
- Market position.

And calling all of it attraction.

Then the conversation gets darker.

## Because eventually people stop talking about attraction altogether and start talking about value.

High-value men.

High-value women.

Low-value men.

Low-value women.

Listen to the language.

**Value**.

Value according to who?

Valuable for what?

Marriage?

Status?

Sex?

Children?

Money?

Attention?

The term sounds precise until you ask one question.

Valuable to which market?

And suddenly the certainty evaporates.

Because value is a market concept.

Attraction is an encounter.

A market asks:

> **“How many people would choose this?”**

An encounter asks:

> **“What is happening between us?”**

Those are not the same question.

Which is why people become trapped.

The person who thinks they’re winning becomes obsessed with maintaining their position.

The person who thinks they’re losing becomes obsessed with improving it.

Desirability squeezes from the top and bottom, this affects women too all the same.

Both become servants of the same scoreboard.

Neither leaves the game.

And then the conversation spreads.

People start talking about races.

Classes.

Nationalities.

Body types.

Height.

Gender.

Entire groups of human beings.

“This is why I only date white women.”

“This is why women want tall men.”

“This is why men want younger women.”

“This is why nobody wants people like me.”

And every single time the same mistake appears.

A market preference becomes a theory of reality.

A trend becomes a law.

An environment becomes human nature.

**Just because a market rewards something does not mean humans are attracted to it.**

**And just because a market ignores something does not mean humans are not.**

I want you to read that again.

Slowly.

Because that single mistake has distorted years of conversation.

A cactus is not defective because it **fails** in the ocean.

A jazz musician is not untalented because a metal festival **won’t book them**.

A person is not unattractive because a particular environment doesn’t reward their traits.

Yet people make that leap every day.

The market didn’t respond.

So they concluded reality didn’t respond.

The environment didn’t choose them.

So they concluded humanity wouldn’t choose them.

A preference appeared.

So they declared a law.

A trend appeared.

So they declared a truth.

An outcome appeared.

So they declared a nature.

That leap is where almost all of the confusion lives.

### Because attraction keeps breaking the theories.

The man who checks every box leaves someone cold.

The woman who fits every beauty standard leaves someone unmoved.

The person who should not matter suddenly matters.

The person who wasn’t supposed to be your type becomes unforgettable.

The hierarchy says no.

The encounter says yes.

And in that moment the market loses jurisdiction.

That’s attraction.

Not the ranking.

Not the score.

Not the likes.

Not the followers.

Not the value.

The moment another human being stops being a category and becomes a person.

The moment the theory breaks.

The moment the market fails.

The moment something real happens.
