Every day, millions of people wake up unable to tell what kind of relationship they’re actually in.

They don’t know if they’re being harmed.

They don’t know if they’re incompatible.

They don’t know if they’re asking too much.

They don’t know if they’re settling.

They don’t know if they’re growing or slowly disappearing.

So they ask the question almost everyone eventually asks.

**“Should I break up?”**

Look closely.

That question is everywhere.

- Friends ask it.
- Married couples ask it.
- People in healthy relationships ask it.
- People in destructive relationships ask it.
- People deeply in love ask it.
- People who have fallen out of love ask it.

Different relationships.

The same question.

That should stop us.

Because questions usually change when reality changes.

This one doesn’t.

Something else has changed instead.

We no longer know what we’re looking at.

That’s the relationship diagnosis crisis.

Love doesn’t primarily fail because people stop caring.

## It fails because modern life continuously inserts additional participants into every relationship.

There is Person A.

There is Person B.

And then there are the sources.

- Algorithms.
- Family histories.
- Trauma.
- Economic pressure.
- Cultural scripts.
- Attachment strategies.
- Fear.
- Comparison.
- Performance.
- Identity.
- Expectation.

Most couples spend their lives arguing with the sources while believing they’re arguing with each other.

Then the relationship develops an audience.

Friends diagnose it.

Family diagnoses it.

Therapists diagnose it.

Books diagnose it.

Podcasts diagnose it.

TikTok diagnoses it.

Comment sections diagnose it.

Soon the relationship is surrounded by explanations before anyone has learned how to explain it.

And that’s when the collapse begins.

Different realities begin sharing the same vocabulary.

Harm becomes **incompatibility.**

Ordinary difference becomes **toxicity.**

Trauma becomes **abuse.**

Abuse becomes an **attachment style.**

Incompatibility becomes a **communication problem.**

Notice what disappeared.

Not relationships.

## Differentiation.

Every diagnosis answers a different question.

Harm asks,

**“Is someone being injured?”**

Trauma asks,

**“What is organizing this person’s reactions?”**

Attraction asks,

**“What qualities is my nervous system recognizing?”**

Compatibility asks something entirely different.

**It asks whether the relationship continually generates contradiction between two ways of being.**

Not whether you love each other.

Not whether you’re attracted to each other.

Not whether you’re both emotionally healthy.

Those questions can all be answered “yes” while the relationship continually produces the same contradiction.

One person continually needs more solitude.

The other continually needs more closeness.

One continually moves toward uncertainty.

The other continually moves toward predictability.

One experiences sacrifice as love.

The other experiences the same sacrifice as disappearance.

Neither person has to become a villain.

Neither person has to become unhealthy.

The relationship simply keeps generating the same contradiction.

That’s why compatibility is so difficult to recognize.

It cannot be diagnosed by ruling it in.

Love doesn’t rule it in.

Chemistry doesn’t rule it in.

Healthy cognition doesn’t rule it in.

Communication doesn’t rule it in.

**Compatibility is diagnosed by ruling everything else out.**

> Is someone causing harm?

Then you’re looking at harm.

> Is someone unwilling to grow?

Then you’re looking at immaturity.

> Is unresolved trauma continually organizing the relationship?

Then you’re looking at trauma.

> Are outside pressures continually reorganizing the relationship?

Then you’re looking at the sources.

Only after those realities have been separated does incompatibility become a meaningful diagnosis.

Compatibility isn’t the first explanation.

**It’s the last one.**

Healthy cognition does not mean compatibility.

You can meet someone emotionally mature...

kind...

honest...

deeply attractive...

and still be incompatible.

We’ve confused attraction with diagnosis.

*“If I’m attracted to someone else, maybe I’m with the wrong person.”*

No.

Attraction tells you another human being possesses qualities your nervous system recognizes.

It tells you nothing about whether your relationship is incompatible.

Those are different questions.

The moment attraction becomes a diagnosis...

every remarkable stranger becomes evidence against your relationship.

The moment ordinary difference becomes harm every disagreement becomes an emergency.

The moment harm becomes incompatibility accountability disappears.

This is why so many people don’t know whether they should stay or leave.

They’re trying to answer a diagnostic question with a collapsed vocabulary.

A civilization that cannot distinguish different relationship realities eventually begins treating every relationship as though it has the same problem.

Once that happens every relationship begins to feel impossible.

Not because love disappeared.

Because our ability to tell different relationship realities apart disappeared first.
