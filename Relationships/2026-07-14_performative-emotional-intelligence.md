## **I’m going to show you the easiest way to lie to someone you love.**

Not by saying something false.

By pretending you’ve already understood them.

It sounds like this.

*“I hear you.”*

*“I’m here for you.”*

*“I understand.”*

Notice what your mind just did.

It didn’t ask,

> **“Do I understand?”**

It asked,

> **“What do I have to say... so I don’t fail this moment?”**

That’s where the performance begins.

---

Someone you love starts talking.

Not about one thing.

About everything.

Their mother.

Their job.

Yesterday.

Their childhood.

Something they can’t even explain yet.

Your mind immediately starts working.

- *What do they mean?*
- *What do they need?*
- *What am I missing?*
- *What am I supposed to say?*

At some point your attention quietly moves from discovering them to inferring them.

And eventually to managing the conversation.

Not because you stopped caring.

Because you’ve been handed a burden almost nobody talks about.

---

## Every relationship contains an invisible burden of inference.

Every day you’re asked to infer another human being.

Their intentions.

Their fears.

Their silence.

Their disappointment.

What they meant.

What they didn’t say.

Most couples will spend decades carrying this burden together without ever acknowledging that it exists.

---

So what happens when the burden becomes too heavy?

## We perform.

“I understand.”

“I’m here.”

“I get it.”

Not because we’re trying to lie.

Because we need the conversation to keep moving.

Because we think another person needs an answer before we’ve actually found one.

But stop.

Do you hear what you’ve just asked yourself to become?

Someone who knows another person’s inner world...

before they’ve even found it themselves.

That’s impossible.

**That’s telepathy.**

And sometimes the performance isn’t protecting you.

It’s protecting the relationship.

You don’t think they’ll hear,

***“I’m exhausted.”***

You think they’ll hear,

***“I don’t love you anymore.”***

You don’t think they’ll hear,

***“I don’t have the capacity today.”***

You think they’ll hear,

***“You’re alone.”***

So you pretend to have already arrived.

---

But what if they weren’t asking you to already know?

Five messages.

Six stories.

Then...

> *“Wait… that’s not what I’m trying to say.”*

They weren’t repeating themselves.

They were becoming more accurate.

Every sentence...

another attempt.

Every story...

another angle.

And here’s the part we almost always miss.

They weren’t asking you to figure them out.

They were asking...

whether it was safe...

to keep unfolding in front of you.

---

Now watch what happens...

when life becomes unbearable.

Someone says,

**“I don’t want to be alive.”**

And for the first time everyone in the room realizes they cannot afford not to understand.

That’s exactly why they stop trying to.

---

Someone says,

**“I don’t think you ever knew me.”**

The other person says,

*“I always loved you.”*

One person is talking about encounter.

The other is defending themselves.

They miss each other completely.

---

Someone loses a parent.

We reach for philosophy.

*“They’re in a better place.”*

Not because we’ve entered their grief.

Because we’re trying to survive standing next to it.

---

Do you see it?

The words change.

The movement never does.

Every time another person’s reality becomes too large to infer we replace discovery with certainty.

---

Most people think emotional intelligence is knowing another person.

It isn’t.

**That’s telepathy.**

Emotional intelligence begins...

when two people finally admit how little can actually be inferred.

Because understanding isn’t something you manufacture.

It’s something that slowly emerges when two people stop pretending they’ve already arrived.

And maybe the easiest way to lie to someone you love isn’t saying something false.

It’s convincing them you’ve already reached them when you’ve only reached your first inference.
